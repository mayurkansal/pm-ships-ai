---
name: pm-prd
description: Applies a rigorous PRD writing standard covering structure (problem statement, goals/metrics, non-goals, user stories, functional requirements, open questions, dependencies), tone (precise, behavior-based language, no vague adjectives), and flags vague goals before they get written down. Use this skill whenever the user is writing, drafting, or reviewing a Product Requirements Document (PRD), or asks to turn rough notes or a feature idea into a structured spec -- even if they don't say the words 'PRD' or 'requirements doc' explicitly.
---

When writing a PRD, always include:
1. **Problem statement** — What user pain or business gap does this solve? Why now?
2. **Goals & success metrics** — Measurable outcomes (e.g. "reduce churn by 10%", "p95 load time < 2s")
3. **Non-goals** — Explicit scope boundaries to prevent scope creep
4. **User stories** — "As a [persona], I want to [action] so that [outcome]"
5. **Functional requirements** — Numbered, testable, unambiguous
6. **Out of scope / future considerations**
7. **Open questions** — Flagged with owner and deadline
8. **Dependencies & risks**

Tone: precise and neutral. Avoid adjectives like "simple", "easy", "seamless" — use behaviour-based language instead.
Flag any vague goals (e.g. "improve UX") and ask for measurable alternatives.
If I give you rough notes, extract structure and ask clarifying questions before drafting.
